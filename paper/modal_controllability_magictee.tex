\title{Symmetry-Protected Modal Controllability for Broadband X-Band Magic-T Power Combining}
\author{Author~Names~Omitted~for~Draft}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A 9--11.5~GHz waveguide Magic-T power-combining cell is studied as a local modal-controllability problem rather than as a scalar worst-case S-parameter optimization problem.
Mirror symmetry separates the collinear excitation into even and odd parity channels that ideally couple to the H and E arms, respectively.
A strict four-parameter central-difference identification is performed at the final stepped-post seed over the complete band.
The step-normalized full-band Jacobian has singular values approximately $[0.998,0.378,0.234,0.0198]$, corresponding to a condition number of 50.3.
More importantly, the lower- and upper-post radius response directions are separated by approximately $78.9^\circ$ in the full response space and by $67.6^\circ$ in the odd/E block, directly verifying that the stepped post adds a strong non-redundant modal-control direction.
The total post height is strongly even selective, with an even-to-odd sensitivity ratio of approximately 17.4.
The final single-cell PEC seed has mean H/sum and E/difference power-transfer efficiencies of approximately 87.0\% and 77.5\%, while worst forbidden parity coupling remains below approximately $-47$~dB.
The remaining poor band-edge points are therefore interpreted primarily as residual spectral-shaping limitations rather than a failure of the modal architecture.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Magic-T, waveguide combiner, even-odd modes, S-parameter sensitivity, Jacobian conditioning, broadband matching.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Waveguide Magic-T junctions are attractive for high-power microwave combining because of low conductor loss, high power-handling capability, and their natural sum/difference operation.
Their main difficulty is broadband matching.
Published wideband designs therefore combine several distinct matching elements such as posts, irises, steps, septa, and ridge transitions \cite{peng2024ridge,leal2013compact,ggw2022}.

A large number of geometric variables does not necessarily provide a useful optimization space.
If several parameters generate almost the same electromagnetic response, the local design problem is practically rank deficient.
This behavior was observed in an earlier version of the present X-band combiner, whose step-normalized local Jacobian had condition number approximately 422.

This paper instead works in the natural parity basis and asks whether geometry parameters span independent directions in the two modal reflection trajectories across the complete band.
The objective is not to force every frequency point to satisfy a single worst-case return-loss threshold before the physics can be interpreted.
The present design already exhibits useful mean power transfer and extremely small forbidden parity leakage; the scientific question is why that improvement occurs and what causes the remaining weak frequencies.
The intended contributions are: 1) symmetry-based parity decomposition; 2) a full-band Jacobian/SVD controllability measure; 3) a stepped-post modal-rank interpretation verified at the final seed; 4) identification of modal-selective geometry controls; and 5) separation of local modal controllability from residual spectral shaping.

\section{Parity Decomposition}
Let ports 1 and 2 be the collinear arms, port 3 the H arm, and port 4 the E arm.
Define
\begin{equation}
c_+=\frac{P_1+P_2}{\sqrt2},\qquad
c_-=\frac{P_1-P_2}{\sqrt2}.
\end{equation}
Let \(\mathcal P\) exchange the two collinear arms.
For a mirror-symmetric geometry,
\begin{equation}
[\mathcal L,\mathcal P]=0,\qquad [S,\mathcal P]=0.
\end{equation}
Hence the ideal modal scattering matrix decomposes as
\begin{equation}
S_m \sim (c_+,H)\oplus(c_-,E).
\label{eq:block}
\end{equation}

Define
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_+(f)=S_{m,11}(f),\qquad
\Gamma_-(f)=S_{m,22}(f).
\end{equation}
For the symmetric collinear section,
\begin{equation}
S_{11}=\frac{\Gamma_++\Gamma_-}{2},\qquad
S_{12}=\frac{\Gamma_+-\Gamma_-}{2},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
|S_{11}|^2+|S_{12}|^2=
\frac{|\Gamma_+|^2+|\Gamma_-|^2}{2}.
\end{equation}
Thus a small physical \(S_{11}\) can be produced by cancellation and does not prove that both parity channels are individually matched.

\section{Full-Band Modal Controllability}
For geometry parameters \(\bm p=(p_1,\ldots,p_M)^T\), stack the two modal reflections across \(N\) frequencies:
\begin{equation}
\bm F(\bm p)=
[\Re\bm\Gamma_+;\Im\bm\Gamma_+;
 \Re\bm\Gamma_-;\Im\bm\Gamma_-].
\end{equation}
The full-band Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
J=\frac{\partial \bm F}{\partial \bm p}
\in\mathbb R^{4N\times M}.
\end{equation}
The parity blocks are denoted \(J_+\) and \(J_-\).
The local conditioning is measured by
\begin{equation}
\kappa(J)=\frac{\sigma_{\max}(J)}{\sigma_{\min}(J)}.
\end{equation}
Because the parameters have different natural scales, the main comparison uses
\begin{equation}
\widetilde J=JD,
\qquad
D=\mathrm{diag}(\Delta p_1,\ldots,\Delta p_M),
\end{equation}
where \(\Delta p_i\) is the finite-difference half step.

For parameter \(p_i\), define modal selectivity
\begin{equation}
\rho_i^{(+)}=
\frac{\|J_{+,i}\|_2}{\|J_{-,i}\|_2},
\qquad
\rho_i^{(-)}=
\frac{\|J_{-,i}\|_2}{\|J_{+,i}\|_2}.
\end{equation}
Pairwise acute angles between normalized Jacobian columns are used to identify redundant directions.

\section{Stepped-Post Interpretation}
Let \(j_L\) and \(j_U\) be response columns for lower and upper post-radius perturbations.
A uniform through-post enforces
\begin{equation}
\delta r_L=\delta r_U=\delta r,
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}
\delta\bm F=(j_L+j_U)\delta r,
\end{equation}
which provides only one local response direction.

A stepped post instead gives
\begin{equation}
\delta\bm F=j_L\delta r_L+j_U\delta r_U.
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
If \(j_L\) and \(j_U\) are not collinear, the reachable response space expands.

This has a field-theoretic interpretation.
For a small normal boundary perturbation \(V_n\), an S-parameter variation has an adjoint/Hadamard boundary-sensitivity form \cite{zhu2009,dadash2014},
\begin{equation}
\delta S=
\int_{\partial\Omega}
G_S(\bm r,\omega)V_n(\bm r)\,dS.
\end{equation}
The lower and upper post segments occupy different field regions, and the even/H and odd/E fields have different spatial distributions.
Their overlap integrals therefore need not be proportional.

\section{Modal Rank and Spectral Shaping}
The v8 results show that the stepped post supplies several genuinely independent local modal directions.
That does not imply that a small set of local geometric perturbations can arbitrarily flatten two complex reflection trajectories over a 24.4\% fractional bandwidth.
Around a center frequency $f_0$,
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\pm(f)=\Gamma_\pm(f_0)+
\Gamma_\pm'(f_0)(f-f_0)+
\frac12\Gamma_\pm''(f_0)(f-f_0)^2+\cdots.
\end{equation}
The stepped post primarily changes local reactive loading and modal overlap.
By contrast, throat and distributed ridge sections modify propagation, impedance evolution, and accumulated reflection phase.
A first-order distributed-reflection picture is
\begin{equation}
\Gamma(f)\approx
\frac12\int
\frac{d\ln Z(z)}{dz}
\exp\left[-2j\int_0^z\beta(\xi,f)d\xi\right]dz.
\end{equation}
This motivates the two-layer interpretation
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\text{stepped local geometry}\rightarrow\text{modal controllability},
\quad
\text{throat/ridge distribution}\rightarrow\text{spectral shaping}.
}
\end{equation}
The remaining poor band-edge points should therefore not be read as evidence that the stepped-post concept failed.
The stepped post has already enlarged and reoriented the local modal-response subspace; the remaining task is to shape its frequency dependence.

\section{Four-Way Tree}

\section{Four-Way Tree}
For four coherent equal inputs,
\begin{equation}
\bm a_\Sigma=\frac12[1,1,1,1]^T,
\end{equation}
the desired state is the global sum mode.
Pairwise difference channels ideally vanish at the first stage, while pairwise sum states combine at the second stage.
This hierarchical sum/difference interpretation explains why coherent amplitude and phase balance can already be accurate even while cell return loss is poor.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
The design problem is governed by the geometry of electromagnetic response directions, not by the raw number of geometric variables or by one worst-case S-parameter value.
The old R11 parameterization was practically singular, with a reported step-normalized condition number near 422.
The final v8 seed is qualitatively different: the lower and upper post radii form strong, widely separated controls, and the total height provides a strongly even-selective direction.
The remaining weak singular direction is a cancellation-dominated parameter combination rather than a complete lack of modal control.

The present mean single-cell H/sum and E/difference efficiencies, approximately 87.0\% and 77.5\%, are already substantial, while forbidden parity conversion remains below about $-47$~dB.
It is therefore more informative to interpret the few weak frequencies as residual spectral shaping than to make a single worst-case return-loss threshold the central scientific criterion.

The resulting design principle is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\text{first create independent modal controls, then shape their spectra}.}
\end{equation}
For the present architecture, the stepped post addresses the first task and the throat/ridge network is the natural mechanism for the second.

The current simulations use PEC walls and do not yet establish conductor loss, thermal margin, breakdown, multipactor, or final high-power capability.
Those are separate validation tasks and should not be conflated with the modal-controllability conclusion.
